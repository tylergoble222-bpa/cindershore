01 / Executive overview

One network. Six arenas. One experience.

Cindershore's current mix of ISP routers, unmanaged switches, and consumer Wi-Fi creates inconsistent performance and makes troubleshooting difficult. The recommended solution standardizes the network across all six sites while giving each arena enough capacity for tournaments, production, guests, and future expansion.

PERFORMANCE

Competitive-grade connectivity

Redundant 100Gbps-capable core switching, 25Gbps minimum internal backbone, wired gaming endpoints, and traffic prioritization help maintain consistent play.

VISIBILITY

Centralized operations

A single cloud-managed platform provides configuration templates, health dashboards, alerting, inventory, and remote troubleshooting for every arena.

RESILIENCE

Designed to stay online

Dual internet providers, high-availability firewalls, redundant core paths, monitored power, and tested failover reduce single points of failure.

Design target: Keep round-trip latency below 5 ms on local wired network segments under normal operating conditions. Internet-game latency depends on the ISP, game server location, and public routing, so it cannot be guaranteed by the local LAN alone.

02 / Network architecture

A fast, segmented foundation.

Each facility uses a resilient core-and-access design. Gaming stations connect over wired Ethernet; Wi-Fi is reserved for devices that need mobility. The internal backbone is designed above the 25Gbps minimum to leave room for broadcast traffic and future station growth.

Core layer

Two stackable or virtual-chassis Layer 3 switches with redundant power and 100Gbps-capable interconnects. Maintain at least 25Gbps usable backbone capacity at each facility.

Access layer

Managed PoE+/PoE++ switches sized for 64 gaming stations, broadcast endpoints, access points, cameras, printers, and spare growth ports.

Wireless layer

Business-grade Wi-Fi 6E/7 access points with controller-managed roaming, channel planning, separate SSIDs, and capacity sized after an RF survey.

Quality of Service (QoS)

Priority 1 — Tournament

Prioritize latency-sensitive game traffic on trusted switch ports. Apply queue scheduling and avoid excessive shaping on the LAN.

Priority 2 — Broadcast

Reserve predictable bandwidth for production and contribution streams; monitor uplink utilization and packet loss.

Priority 3 — Business / VIP

Provide managed bandwidth for staff applications and premium lounge service without starving tournament traffic.

Priority 4 — Guest / bulk

Rate-limit public Wi-Fi and background downloads. Do not allow guest traffic to reach internal subnets.

03 / Centralized management

Operate all locations from one view.

Use a single enterprise networking platform for switch, wireless, firewall, and SD-WAN visibility. Select one vendor ecosystem where practical to simplify support, licensing, and configuration consistency.

Unified dashboard

Live status for WAN links, device health, client counts, AP utilization, interface errors, latency, jitter, packet loss, and bandwidth.

Automated alerting

Notify IT through email or ticketing integration when an ISP fails, a device goes offline, utilization crosses a threshold, or errors rise.

Configuration templates

Standard VLANs, SSIDs, QoS, firewall rules, and firmware policies are centrally maintained and deployed in controlled change windows.

Suggested approach: Evaluate cloud-managed enterprise platforms with multi-site dashboards and SD-WAN capability (for example, Cisco Meraki, Aruba Central, or Juniper Mist). Compare subscription cost, feature licensing, support, and interoperability before selecting a vendor.
04 / Security and remote support

Protect players, staff, and production.

Security is layered across the perimeter, internal network, endpoints, and administrator access. Policies should be consistent at every arena and reviewed regularly.

Next-generation firewalls

Deploy HA firewall pairs with application-aware policies, URL filtering, threat intelligence, and site-to-site encrypted tunnels.

IDS / IPS

Enable intrusion detection and prevention at the perimeter, with tuned rules and centralized event logging to reduce false positives.

DDoS protection

Coordinate upstream DDoS scrubbing and response procedures with both ISPs and game/hosting partners. Keep an incident escalation contact list.

Secure VPN access

Require MFA and device posture checks for remote administration. Use individual accounts; disable shared administrator credentials.

Role-based access

Separate global network administrators, site technicians, security analysts, broadcast operators, and read-only executives. Grant least privilege.

Logging and updates

Centralize firewall, authentication, and network logs. Patch on a schedule, back up configurations, and test restoration procedures.

05 / Scalability and redundancy

Capacity for the next generation.

Build spare capacity into switching, fiber pathways, rack space, power, and addressing so each arena can expand without redesigning the entire network.

Dual ISP uplinks

Use two physically diverse providers where available. Configure automatic failover and test real application behavior during simulated outages.

High-availability core

Use redundant core switches, diverse uplinks, link aggregation where supported, and redundant power supplies on critical equipment.

Double tournament capacity

Reserve rack units, switch ports, fiber strands, IP address space, and uplink bandwidth to scale from 64 toward 128 stations.

Pop-up event venues

Use secure SD-WAN edge kits with preconfigured policies, encrypted tunnels, and temporary connectivity options for satellite events.

Power continuity

Protect core, firewall, and ISP equipment with UPS systems and monitored circuits; size runtime based on site power requirements.

Failover exercises

Run scheduled tests for ISP loss, firewall failover, switch failure, and backup restoration; document results and recovery targets.

06 / Broadcast and streaming

Give every stream a reliable path.

The broadcast studio receives a dedicated, controlled network path that protects production traffic from public Wi-Fi congestion and tournament bursts.

Dedicated production segment

Place cameras, encoders, commentary systems, and production workstations on the broadcast VLAN. Use 10GbE or faster links where device throughput requires it.

Bonded internet uplinks

Evaluate a bonding appliance or managed service to combine available WAN paths for stream resilience. Confirm that the provider supports the required protocol and bandwidth.

CDN delivery

Publish through the chosen streaming platform/CDN. Validate ingest region, bitrate ladder, encoder settings, stream keys, and viewer distribution plan.

Monitoring and isolation

Monitor stream bitrate, dropped frames, packet loss, and WAN headroom. Apply firewall rules to isolate production from guest and IoT networks.

Operational recommendation: Test a full 4K production workflow before opening day, including primary ISP failure, backup uplink behavior, audio/video synchronization, and CDN ingest recovery.

08 / Implementation roadmap

Deploy in controlled phases.

A repeatable deployment process reduces disruption at existing arenas and creates a consistent standard for the 2027 openings.

Phase 1 · Assess

Survey all six sites, document current circuits and equipment, measure RF conditions, and confirm application bandwidth requirements.

Phase 2 · Design

Finalize vendor selection, IP/VLAN plan, firewall policies, fiber paths, rack layouts, ISP diversity, and detailed bill of materials.

Phase 3 · Pilot

Build and test one representative arena. Validate latency, throughput, QoS, failover, security controls, and stream quality.

Phase 4 · Roll out

Upgrade existing arenas in maintenance windows, commission new sites, train staff, and review performance monthly.

Acceptance checks

  • Verify at least 25Gbps internal backbone capacity at every arena.
  • Measure local wired-segment round-trip latency under 5ms during representative load tests.
  • Confirm all VLANs and firewall rules prevent unauthorized cross-segment access.
  • Test dual-ISP failover, firewall high availability, and core-switch redundancy.
  • Run a sustained broadcast test and document bitrate, dropped frames, packet loss, and recovery behavior.
  • Confirm central dashboards, alerts, configuration backups, MFA, and role permissions work at all six sites.
Final recommendation: Approve a site survey and detailed engineering phase first. Use its results to validate the cost range, select the vendor platform, and produce a fixed-price implementation plan for the three new arenas and three existing locations.
